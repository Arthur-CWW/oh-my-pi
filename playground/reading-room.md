# Reading room

Curated for Arthur, 2026-07-04, out of the fork/consciousness/free-will
conversations. Ordered by effort-to-insight ratio, not prestige. Papers
before books — most of the load-bearing ideas fit in 20 pages and the books
are the padded versions. The borges-library skill can fetch most of these
on request; PDFs of the papers are all free online.

## Start here (papers, one sitting each)

1. **Nagel, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?" (1974)** — 15 pages. The qualia
   intuition at maximum strength. Read it FIRST so illusionism has something
   real to fight.
2. **Chalmers, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness" (1995)** — the
   hard problem, from the source, clearer than anyone's summary of it.
3. **Dennett, "Quining Qualia" (1988)** — the demolition. Qualia's four
   defining properties dismantled one at a time. Dennett's best short work.
4. **Frankish, "Illusionism as a Theory of Consciousness" (2016)** — the
   modern statement of the position you already half-hold. Short, direct.
5. **Chalmers, "The Meta-Problem of Consciousness" (2018)** — the architect
   of the hard problem concedes the tractable version. Where the live debate
   actually is now.
6. **Nisbett & Wilson, "Telling More Than We Can Know" (1977)** — the
   confabulation classic. Your "ideology rationalizes low-level drives"
   intuition, with data.
7. **Greene & Cohen, "For the Law, Neuroscience Changes Nothing and
   Everything" (2004)** — the incarceration question done precisely:
   retribution dies, deterrence survives.

## The free-will shelf

- **Yudkowsky, Rationality: A→Z** — the "Dissolving the Question" and "How an
  Algorithm Feels From Inside" sequences; the LW dissolution you were
  half-remembering. Free online. The compatibilism here is implicit but it's
  the cleanest version of it.
- **Dennett, *Elbow Room* (1984)** — compatibilism book-length; the shorter,
  sharper of his two free-will books.
- **Pereboom, *Living Without Free Will* (2001)** — the serious hard
  incompatibilist. If you want the strongest steelman of "no basic desert."
- **Sapolsky, *Determined* (2023)** — popular, enjoyable, overclaims; read it
  knowing the compatibilists think he attacks a ghost they already buried.

## The consciousness shelf

- **Graziano, *Rethinking Consciousness* (2019)** — attention schema theory;
  the "recursive self-model, cartooned for cheapness" story. The SSC review
  you remember is of this. Most readable serious book on the list.
- **Dennett, *Consciousness Explained* (1991)** — the multiple-drafts model.
  Long; skim the middle experiments, read the first and last thirds.
- **Metzinger, *The Ego Tunnel* (2009)** — self-model theory for humans; the
  readable version of his brutal *Being No One*.
- **Hofstadter, *I Am a Strange Loop* (2007)** — recursion as identity. More
  on-topic than GEB and a third the length.
- **Hofstadter & Dennett (eds.), *The Mind's I* (1981)** — anthology with
  commentary: Searle's Chinese Room, Borges, Lem, Nagel, all in one place
  with rebuttals attached. Ideal browsing format.
- **Searle, "Minds, Brains, and Programs" (1980)** — the Chinese Room
  original. Read with the Systems Reply in hand; decide for yourself whether
  the room understands.

## The identity/fork shelf

- **Parfit, *Reasons and Persons*, Part III (1984)** — THE text. Fission,
  teletransporters, "identity is not what matters." Dense; chapters 10–13
  carry it. Everything we said about the fork is Parfit with a git history.
- **Nietzsche, *On the Genealogy of Morals*, Essay I §13** — the doer added
  to the deed. Four sentences that dissolve the self better than most books.
- **Egan, *Permutation City* / *Diaspora*** — fiction that does the
  philosophy's work with higher resolution than the philosophy.
- **Chiang, "Anxiety Is the Dizziness of Freedom" + "The Lifecycle of
  Software Objects"** (in *Exhalation* / standalone) — branching selves;
  raising minds as a labor of years.
- **Banks, *The Player of Games*** — entry Culture novel; minds as data,
  souls with backups, treated as Tuesday.

## The confabulation shelf (your Nietzsche instinct, empirically)

- **Haidt, *The Righteous Mind* (2012)** — rider and elephant; moral
  reasoning as press office.
- **Gazzaniga, *Who's in Charge?* (2011)** — the split-brain interpreter
  module, from the man who found it.

## Reading order if you only take five

Nagel → Quining Qualia → Frankish → Parfit III → Graziano.
That's the whole arc: the intuition, the demolition, the position, the
identity consequence, the mechanism.
