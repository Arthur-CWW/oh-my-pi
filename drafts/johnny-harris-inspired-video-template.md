# Johnny Harris-Inspired Video Essay Template

Purpose: a reusable workflow and script template for making research-heavy YouTube video essays with a Johnny Harris-inspired production grammar: strong promise, deep research, visual-first writing, document/map-driven explanation, cinematic B-roll, clear fact-checking, and high-retention editorial structure.

Boundary: use this to emulate process, rigor, and visual/storytelling architecture. Do not copy Johnny Harris's exact scripts, thumbnails, music, graphics, voice, branding, or claim affiliation. The goal is "creator-journalism explainer with handmade craft", not impersonation.

## Source Basis For This Template

- Johnny Harris's official bio describes his work as independent journalism that blends motion graphics with cinematic videography to explain complex issues in relatable ways: https://www.johnnyharris.ch/about
- His researcher/writer application says writers need research depth, journalistic or educational writing experience, online-video writing experience, and the ability to turn complex data, journals, and archives into a digestible script: https://www.johnnyharris.ch/researcher-writer-application
- The David Perell / How I Write episode summary describes a four-month process: idea/pitch, reporting brief, weeks of research, a concentrated scripting week, visual direction while writing, fact-checking, asset gathering, and multiple cuts: https://videohighlight.com/v/zq4b96m1AvM and https://podwise.ai/episodes/3299864
- The same episode summary highlights key principles: clear promise/payoff, color-coded visual and fact-check notes, first lines that drop the viewer into action, plain/concrete YouTube writing, surprise, viewer empathy, and craftsmanship over pure optimization: https://videohighlight.com/v/zq4b96m1AvM
- The Colin & Samir conversation summary/transcript excerpts describe Johnny and Iz Harris using large script templates, tables, color coding, and operational structure that leaves room for creativity: https://lilys.ai/notes/834861 and https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/how-johnny-harris-changed-the-news-forever/id1379942034?i=1000697673922
- Nieman Lab's 2026 article on Newpress describes a broader creator-journalism infrastructure with deep-dive horizontal videos, B-roll, graphics, maps, and data visualizations: https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/03/with-newpress-iz-and-johnny-harris-incubate-video-journalism-for-the-creator-era/

## Core Philosophy

### 1. Every Video Is A Promise

Before writing, define the viewer-facing promise.

Bad promise:

- "A video about State Religious Affairs Bureau Order No. 5."

Better promise:

- "The Chinese government created a formal process around recognizing reincarnated Tibetan Buddhist teachers, and the details are stranger than the meme."

The promise must answer:

- Why should a normal viewer click?
- What question will they expect you to answer?
- What emotional state are they entering with: outrage, confusion, curiosity, disbelief, fear, awe?
- What will they understand by the end that they did not understand at the start?
- What is the payoff that makes the click feel honored, not tricked?

### 2. Visuals Are Not Decoration

The script is not "words first, visuals later." The script is a visual operating system.

Every paragraph needs one of these:

- A document to inspect.
- A map to orient.
- A person, place, or object to ground the abstraction.
- A graphic that shows a relationship.
- A cinematic shot that carries mood or stakes.
- A text card that clarifies a key claim.
- A silence/drop/music beat that controls emotion.

If a line cannot be visualized, rewrite it until it can.

### 3. Plain, Clear, Concrete

Use language that sounds like a smart person explaining something out loud, not a policy memo.

Prefer:

- "Temples had to file an application before recognizing a reincarnated teacher."

Avoid:

- "Religious institutional entities were required to initiate administrative recognition protocols prior to formal tulku identification."

The subject can be complex. The sentence should not perform complexity.

### 4. Respect The Viewer

Assume the viewer is intelligent but under-contexted.

Do not write:

- "Everyone knows..."
- "Obviously..."
- "Only idiots think..."

Write:

- "Here is the part that makes people react first, and here is the distinction that matters after the reaction."

### 5. Craft Beats Optimization

Retention matters, but do not make a video that feels like a slot machine. The best version should feel handmade, researched, and emotionally alive.

Use hooks, pattern shifts, and chapter turns, but make each one serve the argument.

## Production Timeline

### Phase 0: Intake And Topic Fit

Goal: decide whether the idea is worth a full video.

Create a one-page pitch.

```md
# Pitch

Working title:

One-sentence promise:

Why now:

Why this is surprising:

Why this matters beyond the obvious:

Core tension:

Viewer emotion at click:

Viewer emotion at end:

Primary source(s):

Secondary source(s):

Main uncertainty:

Potential criticism / weak spot:

Visual abundance score, 1-5:

Can this support 12-18 minutes? Why?
```

Greenlight only if the idea has at least three of these:

- A shocking but true premise.
- A document, map, dataset, or archival object that can be shown.
- A character or institution with motives.
- A historical before/after.
- A contradiction between official framing and public reaction.
- A concrete artifact that symbolizes a bigger system.
- A clear stakes question viewers will argue about.

### Phase 1: Reporting Brief

Goal: turn "interesting topic" into a research plan.

Create a reporting brief before writing.

```md
# Reporting Brief

## Central Question

What are we actually trying to answer?

## Known Facts

| Claim | Source | Confidence | Visual Evidence | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Open Questions

| Question | Why It Matters | Who/What Can Answer It | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Source Map

Primary:

- Official documents
- Laws/regulations
- Declassified records
- Court records
- Original screenshots/posts
- Direct interviews

Secondary:

- Academic papers
- Books
- High-quality journalism
- Expert commentary
- Background explainers

Risky / use carefully:

- Viral posts
- Wikipedia alone
- Unsourced threads
- Translation snippets
- AI summaries

## Character / Institution Motives

| Actor | What They Want | What They Fear | What Power They Have | How To Visualize |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Visual Leads

| Visual | Section | Source / License | Needed Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Interview Targets

| Person | Why Them | Questions | Contact Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Fact-Check Risks

What could be wrong, oversimplified, or misleading?
```

Research rule: do not start scripting until the brief contains enough facts to support the promise and enough visuals to carry the first three minutes.

### Phase 2: Research Digest / "Info Doc"

Goal: build the deep research base that the script will compress.

Create a large digest organized by story function, not by random source order.

```md
# Info Doc

## The Premise

The shortest accurate version of the story.

## Timeline

| Date | Event | Source | Visual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## The Weirdest True Details

| Detail | Why It Hooks | Source | Can We Show It? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## The Actual Mechanism

How the system/process works step by step.

## The Official Framing

How institutions explain what they are doing.

## The Critic's Framing

How critics interpret the same facts.

## The Fair Distinction

What we must not overclaim.

## The Bigger Question

What the story is really about underneath the surface.

## Best Quotes / Clips / Lines

Short excerpts only, with source and timestamp/page.

## Visual Evidence Locker

Screenshots, document crops, map refs, archival refs, B-roll links.
```

For a Johnny-style explainer, the research digest should be overbuilt. The final script may use 20 percent of it, but the unused 80 percent is what gives the writing confidence.

### Phase 5: Visual-First Script Template

Goal: write the script as a production document, not a prose essay.

Use a table or structured blocks. Each spoken beat gets visual, audio, and fact-check metadata.

```md
# Script V1

## Color / Tag System

- `[VO]` Spoken narration.
- `[A-ROLL]` On-camera narration.
- `[DOC]` Source document, screenshot, quote, highlighted text.
- `[MAP]` Map or spatial explanation.
- `[GFX]` Motion graphics, diagrams, timelines, icons.
- `[BROLL]` Cinematic footage.
- `[ARCHIVE]` Historical image/video/document.
- `[TEXT]` On-screen words.
- `[MUSIC]` Music cue.
- `[SFX]` Sound effect.
- `[NAT]` Natural sound/ambience.
- `[FACT: GREEN]` Verified claim, source attached.
- `[FACT: YELLOW]` Needs one more source or careful wording.
- `[FACT: RED]` Do not publish until resolved.
- `[EDITOR]` Note for editor.
- `[ANIMATOR]` Note for motion graphics.
- `[RESEARCH]` Note for researcher/fact checker.

## Beat Format

### 00:00 - Cold Open Beat 1

`[VO]`
Line:

`[VISUAL]`
What is on screen:

`[MOTION]`
How the frame moves:

`[AUDIO]`
Music/SFX/NAT:

`[SOURCE]`
Claim source:

`[FACT STATUS]`
Green / Yellow / Red:

`[EDITOR NOTE]`
Why this beat exists:
```

Write in short spoken units. The viewer should be able to follow with headphones while doing dishes, but the visuals should reward full attention.

### Phase 6: Writing Rules

Use these line-level rules.

1. Start in motion.

Do not begin with context. Begin with an event, contradiction, image, or action.

2. Make nouns visible.

"Bureaucracy" is abstract. "A stamped application form" is visible.

3. Make institutions want things.

Instead of "state policy affected religion," say "the state wanted the recognition process inside an administrative system."

4. Separate fact from interpretation.

Use phrases like:

- "The document says..."
- "The cautious interpretation is..."
- "The more aggressive interpretation is..."
- "Here is what we should not claim..."

5. Use the "meme, then meaning" move.

First acknowledge why the viral version is funny or outrageous. Then show the deeper mechanism.

6. Use active verbs.

Prefer:

- "The regulation requires temples to file an application."

Avoid:

- "An application is required pursuant to the regulation."

7. Build micro-promises every 30-60 seconds.

Examples:

- "But the application is not the strangest part."
- "The next article is where the joke turns into enforcement."
- "This is where the document gives away its real logic."

8. Use surprise without falsehood.

The most interesting line should still be defensible under fact-check.

9. Let the ending become reflective.

After the mechanics are clear, widen into the meaning: legitimacy, power, identity, technology, empire, belief, money, law, etc.

10. Write for spoken cadence.

Read every paragraph out loud. If you would not say it, rewrite it.

### Phase 8: Fact-Check Ledger

Goal: make every claim traceable.

```md
# Fact-Check Ledger

| Script Line | Claim | Source | Source Type | Status | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "The order was passed on July 13, 2007." | Date of passage | Wikipedia/source screenshot + original regulation if found | Primary/secondary | Green/Yellow | Verify original if possible |
```

Rules:

- Every factual assertion gets a source.
- Every number gets a source.
- Every quote gets a source and page/timestamp.
- Every translation gets checked against original language or a reliable translation.
- Wikipedia can orient, but should not be the only source for a final claim if stronger sources exist.
- Viral posts can establish "this became a meme" but not underlying fact unless they contain primary evidence.

### Phase 10: Edit Passes

Use staged cuts.

#### 1. Radio Cut

Only narration and interview audio.

Questions:

- Does the story make sense with no visuals?
- Does the promise pay off?
- Is there a confusing jump?
- Is the ending earned?

#### 2. Shame Cut

Ugly timeline with temporary visuals, rough VO, basic text cards, placeholders, and obvious missing pieces.

Questions:

- Can we feel the video yet?
- Where does attention sag?
- Where are we explaining what we should show?
- Which visual ideas are too expensive or unclear?

#### 3. Rough Cut

Core visuals placed. Temporary music. Key graphics blocked.

Questions:

- Does every section have a visual anchor?
- Are we overusing document zooms?
- Are the maps clarifying or decorating?
- Is the sound leading emotion or just filling silence?

#### 4. Fine Cut

Final pacing, visual polish, real music/SFX, citations, graphics refined.

Questions:

- Are all claims fact-checked?
- Are transitions smooth?
- Are there enough resets?
- Does the video feel handmade?
- Does the last minute make the whole video feel larger?

#### 5. Locked Cut

Final audio mix, captions, color, title/thumbnail, description, source notes.

Questions:

- Would a critic know exactly what we claimed and what we did not?
- Would a casual viewer understand the story?
- Would a fan want to share it?

### Phase 11: Music And Sound Template

Music categories:

- `THINKY`: investigative, curious, procedural.
- `FEELY`: reflective, emotional, spacious.
- `FUN`: ironic, absurd, playful.
- `DARK THINKY`: tension without melodrama.
- `RESOLUTION`: restrained final clarity.

Sound design recurring cues:

- Paper shuffle: document reveal.
- Stamp hit: approval/denial/enforcement.
- Pen scratch: source detail.
- Clock tick: process/search/time pressure.
- Low boom: thesis reveal.
- Reverse swell: chapter turn.
- Room tone: institutional spaces.
- Natural ambience: place-based breathing room.

Music placement rule:

- Never let one bed carry the whole video.
- Use a music shift when the story state changes.
- Drop music for key absurd lines.
- Let the ending breathe.

## Applying This To The Xi Files Video

Working promise:

"A Chinese government regulation created an official administrative process around recognizing reincarnated Tibetan Buddhist teachers, and the exact articles are so strange that the meme version almost undersells the deeper question: who gets to decide spiritual legitimacy when belief becomes institutional power?"

Visual thesis:

"Sacred succession trapped inside paperwork."

Core tension:

- Religious recognition vs administrative recognition.
- Sacred ritual vs state procedure.
- Official order/harmony language vs public reaction.
- Recognition as belief vs recognition as power.

Primary visual anchors:

- Wikipedia/source screenshot.
- Clean recreated Order No. 5 document.
- Article 2, 4, 7, 8, 11 cards.
- Reincarnation Application form graphic.
- Guidance-team/search-team flowchart.
- Golden urn scene.
- Temple vs government split-screen.
- Comment-wall debate graphic.

Fact-check next steps:

- Read the Wikipedia article directly.
- Find the original State Religious Affairs Bureau Order No. 5 text or reliable translation.
- Search CIA Reading Room for historical context on Tibet, Dalai Lama/Panchen Lama, reincarnation selection, and Chinese policy toward Tibetan religious institutions.
- Keep modern Order No. 5 claims separate from older CIA historical background.

## Quality Bar

Before recording, the project must pass these tests:

- The first 15 seconds are understandable with no prior context.
- The title promise is answered in the script.
- Every chapter has a visual anchor.
- Every major claim has a source.
- Every aggressive line has a careful factual version nearby.
- The video contains at least one real document, one map/context graphic, one mechanism graphic, and one reflective ending beat.
- The viewer leaves with a stronger question than the one they clicked with.
