## standalone longpost — 2061569103903477843
- **Date:** 2026-06-01
- **Status URLs (in order):** https://x.com/pleometric/status/2061569103903477843
- **Source:** [pleometric-longposts.md §39](../pleometric-longposts.md#39-standalone-longpost-2061569103903477843)

> For every viral AI video, there is a similarly viral anti-AI one, usually in vlog format. These videos speaking out against AI generated content also have similarly high engagement comments.
> This particular video is pointing to a trend where users insert themselves in candid "jumbotron footage". The main contention of the video is that it is declassé to participate in AI viral trends, and comments often add that it is also unethical because "AI wastes water". This has become a popular catch all angle for why AI usage is bad on an objective level, on top of being low status.
> It was originally based on "Korean Baseball (KBO) camera footage" videos and then expanded to include other sports and nationalities, like American basketball and etc. It has since gone viral in multiple countries across short form platforms.
> A few directional observations:
> Judging by country of origin of both commenters and posters, the water discourse is concentrated in North America, but you'll also find accounts in Europe, South America and SEA repeating that point.
> There seems to be a gender and age divide in positive attitudes towards AI content. People who enjoy AI content seem to be both very young and very old, while millennials and elder gen-z appear to be the loudest opponents. Positive attitudes seem to come mostly from male accounts, but that is hard to verify for videos that I do not have access to interaction data, and I'm going mostly from my own big viral ones.
