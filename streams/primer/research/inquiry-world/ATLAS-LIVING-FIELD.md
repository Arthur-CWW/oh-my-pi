# Atlas as a Living Field

## The inheritance

Atlas is one continuous place, not a menu of visualizations. Its job is to let a person inhabit a body of inquiry while preserving the difference between authored structure, source-derived material, editorial guidance, learner work, and procedural candidates. The field may become abundant or austere, but it must not fork into parallel products or imply facts that the records do not declare.

This prototype inherits the existing Atlas: continuous pan and zoom, authored placement, explicit regions, typed relations, source recovery, minimap orientation, keyboard movement, synchronized ledger, review state, authorship, and provenance. The new controls alter display behavior only. They do not alter the workspace.

## Operational critique

A map can become sterile when every object is reduced to an identical labeled box. It can become over-administered when understanding requires opening a drawer for every local question. It can become over-resolved when the interface treats one editorial route as the territory itself. It can become closed and low-entropy when peripheral material disappears until explicitly requested. It can become low-risk and insufficiently negotiable when there is only one sanctioned density, one hierarchy, and one way to look.

The response is not decorative chaos. It is controlled permeability:

- useful text is available to peripheral vision;
- local relations can temporarily bloom around a focus;
- attention changes emphasis without rewriting placement;
- movement leaves a faint, erasable residue;
- structural layers can be turned down without being confused with deletion;
- ontology filters fade rather than erase.

The field should feel handled and revisable, not randomly generated. Every visible relation remains declared. Every region remains authored. Every echo points back to an existing neighbor.

## Influences

### Borges: the map as an inexhaustible reading instrument

Borges contributes recursion, adjacency, and the sense that any fragment may open another chamber. Atlas borrows the library as topology rather than as a shelf. It rejects Borges as an excuse for infinite content or mystification: the field is finite, inspectable, and provenance-bearing.

### Warburg: montage as a method of thought

Warburg’s panels make meaning through charged vicinity. Separation and proximity are arguments without becoming proofs. Atlas inherits the possibility that heterogeneous forms can share a surface and that the eye can compare before the hand commits. It does not infer affinity from proximity: placement semantics and relations remain explicit.

### Alice: scale as cognition

Alice’s changing scale is a model for semantic fisheye. Attention may enlarge a thing and change what becomes legible around it. The world does not become a new world; the terms of access change. The effect should be curious and spatial, never elastic spectacle.

### Matuschak: notes that remain navigable objects

Matuschak’s work contributes short, legible conceptual handles, meaningful links, and movement through a graph by following thought-sized objects. Atlas extends this into a heterogeneous world: source shards, questions, tensions, examples, mechanisms, artifacts, and rooms should not collapse into one note-card grammar.

## The Living Field principle

**One canonical inquiry world, many transient degrees of visual abundance.**

A Living Field responds to attention without pretending attention changes truth. Selection, zoom, layer visibility, ontology fades, adjacency echoes, and traversal residue are local display state. They are negotiable lenses over durable records.

The user should be able to move from an organic reading—with body cues, neighbors, regions, roads, frontiers, and residue—to a stripped structural reading in seconds. Both are readings of the same Atlas. Neither writes epistemic state.

## Feature semantics and defaults

### Peripheral text — on

Placed objects show short body fragments or role cues before selection. The fragments are deliberately clipped and ontology-sensitive. A source shard may read like an excerpt; a question like an opening; a mechanism like a compact operation. The purpose is pre-attentive usefulness, not miniature full documents.

### Adjacent bloom — on

Selecting an object creates adjacency echoes around it for direct, relation-backed neighbors. Each echo names the typed relation, the neighbor, and a short cue. Clicking an echo navigates to that existing neighbor.

Echoes are not new records, inferred recommendations, or canonical placements. They are temporary local projections labeled **adjacency echo**. The authored map remains beneath them.

### Semantic fisheye — on

The selected object and immediate neighbors become more legible in place. Scale and visual priority may expand; nearby display geometry may gently relax. Canonical placement is not mutated. Fisheye is an attention field, not a spatial edit.

### Traversal residue — on

Recent selections form a faint labeled polyline. It answers “where have I just moved?” rather than “what does the system think I mastered?” The trail is transient, local, and clearable. It is spatial residue, never analytics or assessment.

### Regions — on

Authored inquiry territories, boundary character, and entry questions remain visible. Turning regions off removes their rendered layer only; membership records remain intact.

### Typed roads — on

Declared relations render as roads. Relation type and label remain available as semantics. Turning roads off suppresses this layer, not the relations in the ledger or workspace.

### Frontiers — on

Open questions and frontier treatments remain visible. A frontier means unresolved or explicitly open according to the record. It does not mean low quality or inferred uncertainty.

### Editorial route — off

An authored route is optional guidance through the territory. When off, its line, step markers, and HUD do not occupy the field. When on, it remains clearly editorial rather than canonical or compulsory.

### Ontology-kind fade — all active

Kind chips control emphasis. An inactive kind and the roads incident to it fade but remain spatially present. Fade preserves orientation, prevents a filter from masquerading as deletion, and makes comparison reversible.

## Epistemic invariants

1. There is one Atlas route and one canonical workspace.
2. Controls are transient display state, never durable epistemic truth.
3. Authored placement remains authored placement.
4. Fisheye and bloom never write placement geometry.
5. Adjacency echoes are generated only from declared immediate relations.
6. Echoes are labeled as echoes and never presented as new records.
7. Road labels retain declared relation type and wording.
8. Ontology filters fade; they do not remove records or imply absence.
9. Route guidance remains editorial and defaults off.
10. Review state, authorship, confidence, provenance, and source locators are preserved.
11. Unsupported claims remain visibly unsupported.
12. Traversal history is not mastery, importance, popularity, or analytics.
13. Source recovery opens retained locators rather than manufacturing source wording.
14. The ledger remains synchronized with the same underlying representation.
15. Pan, zoom, minimap, and keyboard movement remain continuous access to one world.

## Experiments to run through toggles

### Abundant field

Leave every default on. Select a concept, a source shard, a tension, and an open question. Ask whether peripheral cues make the next move plausible without opening the inspector. Ask whether echoes feel like local possibility rather than recommendations.

### Bare structure

Turn off peripheral text, bloom, fisheye, residue, regions, and frontiers. Keep typed roads on. Evaluate whether the relational skeleton is legible and whether faded ontology kinds preserve enough orientation.

### Territory without roads

Keep regions, frontiers, and peripheral text on; turn roads off. Test whether authored territories and object character support exploratory reading without line congestion.

### Roads without territory

Turn regions off and typed roads on. Compare the relational topology to the territorial reading. Look for cases where a road label is insufficiently specific or a relation type is doing too much work.

### Local montage

Keep bloom and fisheye on while turning peripheral text off. Select objects with many kinds of neighbors. Test whether adjacency echoes form a useful Warburg-like local panel while remaining unmistakably temporary.

### Peripheral-only reading

Keep peripheral text on and bloom off. Pan without selecting. Identify which ontology kinds need a different cue length, voice, shape, or type treatment.

### Frontier pressure

Toggle frontiers repeatedly while keeping regions and roads stable. Check that unresolved material changes emphasis without making settled structure appear more certain than its review state warrants.

### Editorial contrast

Turn the route on only after free exploration. Compare the route to the traversal residue. The former must read as authored guidance; the latter as the visitor’s recent path. They should never be visually or conceptually interchangeable.

### Ontology negotiation

Fade one kind at a time, then several together. Watch whether incident roads recede coherently and whether the remaining field invites a different question without suggesting filtered objects ceased to exist.

## Design vocabulary

**Living Field** — the single malleable spatial representation.

**Display state** — transient visual configuration with no epistemic authority.

**Canonical placement** — authored object geometry retained by the workspace.

**Peripheral cue** — clipped, object-specific text available without selection.

**Adjacency echo** — temporary, relation-backed projection of an existing neighbor near the current focus.

**Bloom** — the moment local declared neighbors become directly actionable around a focus.

**Semantic fisheye** — local enlargement and emphasis according to attention, without placement mutation.

**Traversal residue** — faint, erasable geometry of recent movement.

**Typed road** — a visible declared relation whose type and label remain available.

**Region** — authored territory with explicit membership or boundary semantics.

**Frontier** — explicitly unresolved inquiry material, not inferred uncertainty.

**Editorial route** — authored sequence offered as guidance, never imposed as the map.

**Ontology fade** — reversible reduction of emphasis that retains spatial presence.

**Abundance** — useful simultaneous legibility, not maximal decoration.

**Structural view** — reduced display emphasizing topology and placement.

**Negotiability** — the ability to alter visual abundance without altering the underlying claims.

**Low-entropy interface** — a surface whose over-resolution leaves too few plausible readings or moves.

**Source recovery** — return from a derived object to its retained authored context.

## Character of the field

The field should feel like a map annotated by many careful hands: uneven in a purposeful way, dense where inquiry is dense, quiet where structure needs air. It should reward looking before clicking. Selection should make the local world more articulate, not replace it with a panel. Controls should remain glanceable and modest in footprint. Reduction should feel like tuning an instrument, not entering a different application.

The measure of success is not how many layers are visible. It is whether a person can compare, orient, follow, doubt, and recover sources while always knowing which semantics belong to the records and which belong only to the current view.
