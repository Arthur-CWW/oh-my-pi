# Wonderland Cartography: a live exploration

## Hypothesis

The Atlas can become stranger without becoming less truthful. Wonderland is a visual grammar for encountering one finite, authored inquiry world at several conceptual scales at once: a question may behave like a gate, a source like a weighty territory, and a room like a threshold one can cross. The surprise should come from how known things meet—not from invented content, concealed navigation, or fantasy scenery.

This pass keeps the Working Paper grammar intact and places Wonderland beside it as a transient switch. The workspace, placements, relations, feature flags, review states, and route all remain the same. The wager is that changing silhouette, rhythm, depth, and route character can invite less linear reading while typed labels and authorship marks continue to say exactly what the records say.

## Influence becoming behavior

### Borges — finite recursion, visible return

Borges appears as fragments that continue beyond the paper, roads that seem to leave one scale and return at another, and labels which suggest that the margin is not the end of the thought. Nothing is generated behind those hints. Every actionable entrance resolves to an existing entry question, and every road is still a declared relation. The labyrinth is a way of looking at a bounded corpus, not a promise of infinity.

### Alice — scale-slip as attention

Alice appears in the unequal silhouettes. The focus question becomes a broad landmark; a source shard gains the physical gravity of a retained document; intuitions contract into quick handles; open questions become unusually porous thresholds. The scale changes do not claim importance or certainty. They articulate modes of access and make the field feel inhabited rather than normalized.

### Warburg — charged vicinity without inferred affinity

Warburg appears in simultaneous legibility: heterogeneous objects share a field, source texture remains visibly different from learner work, and peripheral fragments keep distant thought available to the eye. Proximity alone still asserts nothing. Curved roads retain their type and wording, while adjacency echoes identify themselves as temporary, relation-backed projections.

### Matuschak — thought-sized handles and multiple entrances

Matuschak appears in readable object titles, short peripheral cues, and direct movement from a relation or threshold into an existing thought. A region’s threshold and the object itself are two entrances to the same question. The handles differ in shape and scale, but they never stop being inspectable objects with source, review, and authorship cues.

## Vocabulary observed in the live grammar

**Scale-slip** is the strongest departure from Working Paper. Object kinds no longer queue for equal rectangular status.

**Threshold** is literal and named: each visible region offers an `ENTER ?` arch derived from its entry question. It is not a hidden shortcut.

**Fold** appears through curved, variably articulated roads and through labels that sit at oblique edges of the field. The fold changes how a relation travels, never which objects it connects.

**Portal** is a second entrance into an existing question, not a route to a separate scene.

**Parallax** separates near notation, the placed world, and far marginal fragments during pan. It gives the canvas depth while leaving object coordinates untouched.

**Impossible road** means a road may curve with diagrammatic wit rather than obeying office-chart orthogonality. Its arrow and always-visible typed label preserve direction and meaning.

**Authored irregularity** means a small number of local rotations, skewed tensions, odd corners, doubled source borders, and unequal shadows. The pattern is tied to object role rather than random variation.

**Peripheral continuation** keeps human-scale phrases beyond the paper’s edges. They are atmosphere and orientation, deliberately non-actionable; actionable thresholds remain explicit.

## Deliberate risks

- Large landmarks may overlap neighboring objects at particular zooms. The field currently privileges semantic scale contrast; live review should test whether any collision blocks selection or relation reading.
- Always-visible road labels are more honest than hover-only labels, but dense crossings may create typographic traffic. Their paper-colored stroke is intended to keep wording readable without turning each label into a card.
- The saturated coral focus question can dominate too strongly. It should announce an entrance, not imply evidential strength.
- Curved roads may look more causal than their relation type warrants. Variation in line vocabulary helps, but the label has to remain the authority.
- Parallax can feel like depth or like drift. Reduced motion removes it completely while retaining every fragment and threshold.
- The portal silhouette is intentionally theatrical. If it reads as fantasy scenery rather than a visible interaction contract, it should become more diagrammatic rather than more subtle.

## Still undecided

The grammar does not yet decide whether Wonderland should inherit application light/dark themes or remain intrinsically sunlit and mineral. It does not decide whether scale-slip should respond continuously to semantic zoom, or remain kind-authored as it is here. The exact density of peripheral fragments, whether roads should carry repeated arrowheads, and how an expanded room should alter nearby perspective all need observation rather than policy. We also have not settled whether a threshold belongs to every region or only to regions whose entry question creates a meaningful conceptual change.

## Six questions for Arthur after live review

1. When you first switch to Wonderland, what becomes newly tempting to click—and is that temptation epistemically useful?
2. Does the large coral focus question feel like a conceptual landmark, or does it falsely read as the most important or most certain object?
3. Do the `ENTER ?` thresholds make multiple entrances legible, or do they feel too theatrical for this body of inquiry?
4. Which road feels most like a productive fold, and which one becomes harder to read than its Working Paper counterpart?
5. Do the source, editorial, and learner materials remain distinct at a glance, or does the Wonderland materiality blur one of those authorship differences?
6. After panning into the periphery and returning, does the depth help spatial memory—or does it make the world feel less anchored?
