# Atlas Style Board

## Purpose and sequence

This board is a live comparative specimen, not four product proposals. Every panel renders the same seven object kinds and the same typed relations so visual grammar—not content or capability—is the variable.

**Functionality will be chosen first; skins come later.** The Living Field’s interaction and epistemic contracts must be settled independently of a visual skin. This moodboard supports learning and naming design vocabulary. It does not commit architecture, fork Atlas, or apply a style to the production Atlas.

## Nocturnal Mnemosyne (`mnemosyne`)

**Influence → behavior**

- Relational montage → distant objects remain visually available so affinity can precede a linear explanation.
- Astronomical notation → sparse points, orbits, and typed rays make recurrence and distance readable.
- Archival light tables → source shards appear as illuminated evidence within a deep negative field.

**Vocabulary:** constellation, montage, afterimage, index, orbit, negative field.

**Borrow:** relational juxtaposition, variable density, luminous indexing, generous darkness.

**Do not copy:** any particular Warburg panel, archival photograph, celestial chart, or recognizable plate arrangement. Do not let visual proximity imply an undeclared relation.

**Makes easy to perceive:** recurrence, distant affinities, and the source fragments that haunt a concept.

**Risk:** atmosphere can outrun legibility; weak relations may appear more authoritative than they are. Dark fields can also conceal low-contrast labels.

## Paper Labyrinth (`paper`)

**Influence → behavior**

- Annotated manuscripts → authorship and revision remain visible through marginal marks rather than being flattened into a clean final state.
- Field notebooks → provisional conjectures read as situated observations.
- Editorial proofing → typed marks distinguish correction, question, source, and proposed connection.
- Layered maps → overlap records history without requiring every layer to be equally prominent.

**Vocabulary:** palimpsest, marginalia, crease, rubric, trace, overprint.

**Borrow:** evidence of revision, tactile hierarchy, editorial marks, warm ground, irregular registration.

**Do not copy:** historical manuscript pages, facsimile handwriting, proprietary map styles, or decorative aging. Material cues must explain state, not simulate authenticity.

**Makes easy to perceive:** authorship, revision, source texture, and the provisional status of a claim.

**Risk:** dense annotation can become nostalgic clutter, reduce scan speed, and obscure the live structure.

## Living Diagram (`living`)

**Influence → behavior**

- Developmental biology → regions behave like membranes and branching systems rather than fixed containers.
- Cybernetic diagrams → feedback and causal direction are explicit, typed, and inspectable.
- Procedural drawing → geometry can respond continuously to focus and traversal.
- Process philosophy → objects read as temporarily stabilized processes, not inert cards.

**Vocabulary:** membrane, pulse, branching, metabolism, gradient, feedback.

**Borrow:** legible flow, responsive boundaries, restrained pulse, growth direction, local feedback.

**Do not copy:** scientific illustrations, cellular forms as decoration, or motion that claims life, agency, confidence, or causality not present in the data.

**Makes easy to perceive:** causal movement, active tensions, feedback, and where inquiry is still growing.

**Risk:** motion can imply agency or certainty that the evidence does not support. It must yield to reduced-motion preferences and never become ambient noise.

## Wonderland Cartography (`wonderland`)

**Influence → behavior**

- Impossible maps → a conceptual fold can expose alternate adjacency without pretending to be geographic truth.
- Theatrical perspective → foreground and distance become authored emphasis.
- Scale play → a source can become a territory while a broad concept contracts to a landmark.
- Diagrammatic fiction → surprise invites exploration while typed roads preserve explicit semantics.

**Vocabulary:** threshold, fold, scale-slip, portal, parallax, impossible road.

**Borrow:** productive disorientation, scale contrast, spatial wit, multiple entrances, visible thresholds.

**Do not copy:** recognizable fantasy illustration, named fictional geography, optical-illusion artwork, or ornamental quotation. Never hide navigation behind unexplained portals.

**Makes easy to perceive:** surprise, shifts of scale, conceptual thresholds, and multiple ways into a question.

**Risk:** playful distortion can weaken orientation, make navigation feel arbitrary, or turn inquiry into spectacle.

## Lightweight comparison rubric

Score each grammar from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong), using the same content and the same task. Record one sentence of observed evidence for every score.

| Criterion | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Object distinction | Can concept, source, intuition, mechanism, tension, open question, and learner conjecture be distinguished without a legend hunt? |
| Relation honesty | Are typed roads legible without implying unlabeled or stronger relations? |
| Source and authorship | Can a viewer tell sourced material from learner conjecture and editorial framing? |
| Orientation | Can the viewer locate the landmark, frontier, and a return path within seconds? |
| Productive ambiguity | Does the field stay negotiable without becoming vague or low-information? |
| Comparative temperature | Does the grammar reveal something the other three do not, beyond palette? |
| Accessibility | Do focus, contrast, reduced motion, and text legibility survive the grammar? |
| Durability | Could the vocabulary guide many states without becoming a decorative theme? |

A promising direction should reveal inquiry structure while resisting sterile, over-administered, over-resolved, closed, low-entropy, or insufficiently negotiable presentation. Selection should follow task trials and interaction design—not visual preference alone.
