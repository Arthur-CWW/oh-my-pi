### From “Enabling environment”

Source URL: https://notes.andymatuschak.org/z492hGrHvRvJiEY9UfB4Mby

> An *enabling environment* significantly expands its participants’ capacity to do things they find meaningful and important.

> Schools ostensibly aspire to this purpose, but [Educational objectives often subvert themselves](/z2pjEUvJy7Ge6yDaBo66zEu) in large part because [Enabling environments’ activities directly serve an intrinsically meaningful purpose](/z3PJFWDZ7gar2ttawQTmBek). In general, [Enabling environments focus on creating opportunities for growth and action, not on skill-building](/z7d63BYfJrd81VFE25jkcDd).

> Many other social institutions represent powerful enabling environments. Highly functional corporations are often great examples of enabling environments. In these organizations, new employees might feel far more personally capable than they ever had before, even after many years of experience. Likewise, [Y Combinator](/z9MxCsvv8DcHndqRECbxrRi) is an enabling environment.

> Great software environments are enabling environments. Photoshop expands experts’ range of artistic expression and unlocks previously-rarefied photo enhancement techniques for novices. Software development tools enable teenagers to make games and distribute them to millions at zero marginal cost. By contrast, [Most games aren’t enabling environments](/zS7EYYnBEDPVcYdqCRTCi5Q), and [Educational games are a doomed approach to creating enabling environments](/zUVBJdPc4kBud5fsLmPFpbw).

> Books and videos rarely deliver here: [Mass mediums are typically bad at helping people translate ideas to practice](/zHjz9JSan5WNzDR7MXxjLix).

> A collection of densely-connected [Evergreen notes](/z5E5QawiXCMbtNtupvxeoEX) can be an enabling environment for the author: [Evergreen note-writing helps insight accumulate](/z4EXkuLjdBrBZe7PVAGXc5a). (See also [Evergreen note-writing as fundamental unit of knowledge work](/zR6RRbCfY5rFkiimFnaJZKB))

### Designing enabling environments

Source URL: https://notes.andymatuschak.org/z492hGrHvRvJiEY9UfB4Mby

> Enabling environments are generally authored, but [Powerful enabling environments usually arise as a byproduct of projects pursuing their own intrinsically meaningful purposes](/z2huUCj3ko99HdzFcmEDfZD). [Authored environments are significantly colored by authors’ motivations](/z4wZFERkVVVVy6bN6BE8kQz); that often means [Powerful enabling environments focus on expert use](/zY3aLuvtsYS54QnymGKFGwg).
>
> [Designing new enabling environments can be framed as designing a University++](/zyjePyZ77P2BfdkRBuQB2x)
>
> Challenges in authoring enabling environments:
>
> - [Enabling environments focus on doing what’s enabled](/z2etsLyP1LJUwNDPCwvRdUG)

>
> - design representations which expand the range of action for a participant’s existing expertise / efforts; e.g.:

>   - checklists in airplanes/hospitals

### From “§Enabling environments, games, and the Primer”

Source URL: https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zGSGS1UHDogPKtvZB5hdT2A

> **(This turned into a full essay [here](https://andymatuschak.org/primer). These notes are now pretty outdated.)**
>
> Rather than focusing on the Primer, I’m finding a more powerful framing to be in centering the essay around scaling high-growth environments.

> - You can read a book about starting a startup, or you can join [Y Combinator](/z9MxCsvv8DcHndqRECbxrRi). Which do you choose? This is the power of an [Enabling environment](/z492hGrHvRvJiEY9UfB4Mby).
>   - The latter’s a much more effective growth environment, and it’s mostly not the funding.
>   - When you read the book, you’re a founder wanna-be reading about the activities of founders, and you’re not “practicing founding”; when you do YC, you’re *doing founding,* in a community of founders. The book’s abstract, but YC’s not: every lesson is in the context of the idea you care deeply about:
>   - [Enabling environments’ activities directly serve an intrinsically meaningful purpose](/z3PJFWDZ7gar2ttawQTmBek)
>   - [Enabling environments focus on doing what’s enabled](/z2etsLyP1LJUwNDPCwvRdUG)
>   - [Enabling environments focus on creating opportunities for growth and action, not on skill-building](/z7d63BYfJrd81VFE25jkcDd)
>   - The difference isn’t just emotional: you’re going to learn this material much more effectively in the context of YC because it’s a [Participatory environment](/zRWEWHx4cQyqQWRh26gp7ad): [Participatory environments support learning](/zU3Cw3dParuur9TFVNJMiqS)
>   - The format also allows its designers to author founders’ experience over a multi-month period, while [Mass mediums mostly lack an authored time dimension beyond a day](/zBcm1RNbzedjDMTt8rRxoWt)
>   - The authentic purpose doesn’t just improve the experience for participating founders: it also helps YC’s creators design much better systems.
>   - [Powerful enabling environments usually arise as a byproduct of projects pursuing their own intrinsically meaningful purposes](/z2huUCj3ko99HdzFcmEDfZD)
>   - [Effective system design requires insights drawn from serious contexts of use](/z7EQ2nVGus5B1rS9CqT18g6)
>   - Of course, YC’s lessons aren’t “lessons” in a traditional sense—they’re experienced as the natural consequence of your actions in that environment: YC is an [Enacted experience](/z92TGMiBsnraf5KXxSTNkBJ).
>   - Even though you don’t know what you’re doing as a founder ([Novices in enabling environments often can’t do what’s enabled](/zPrYC99kH227c1otipAefyQ)), YC’s systems give you enough support to participate authentically and hone your skills in this intense context: [Enacted experiences can bootstrap active participation in enabling environments](/zKubRcJXKjiCDPsPgJWckkP)
>   - But those experiences aren’t an accident: YC’s enacted lessons are carefully *authored*, in the same sense that a book is. [Enacted experiences can create intense personal connection to authored targets](/zJxSaJaCeXZsY7pH2Q6t89t); [Enacted experiences amplify the power of narrative](/zQ9BWTY2JK6eJvCSqupc7UF).

### Enacted experiences, games, and educational-game tension

Source URL: https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zGSGS1UHDogPKtvZB5hdT2A

> - Unfortunately, [Enacted experiences are hard to author](/z8r1EZL3sFrFe7brjiwFbFN) and [Enacted experiences are hard to distribute](/zGNVRh1q5gGeF88PbmYu4BB).
>   - But there’s hope: [Enacted experiences have incredible potential as a mass medium](/z984ZZLbG4wREM6HAZAovMB)
> - Video game design is the best prior art we have for this.
>   - (some core notes on games and enacted experiences in [Enacted experience](/z92TGMiBsnraf5KXxSTNkBJ); needs to be refactored)
>   - emotional experiences and narrative unfold as a consequence of player action
>   - and they’re good at building skills: [Games effectively develop players’ skills](/z9VDvQzzC4JD4DBWMVaVGjk)
>   - games demonstrate how dynamic media can do things that books can’t do: [Games help players evaluate their developing skills](/zJP88EJ3qnPdZLMFK9g4Boh) and [Games help players make and adapt plans](/z8cCUawpidfMcH7bYE67CEf)
>   - but games are an aesthetic medium; they’re about [“Find the fun”](/zHAGZzVW1jygpT2SoFXZwSA); they’re not externally-oriented like non-fiction and YC: [Skill development in games is subservient to other intrinsically meaningful purposes](/zKt9wffdgJVT97ca4roAUWD)
> - this is the core mistake that educational games make
>   - [Educational games try to teach through enacted experiences](/zB3bu9n53v6UCnqUYKxdAtG)
>   - [Educational games are a doomed approach to creating enabling environments](/zUVBJdPc4kBud5fsLmPFpbw)

### Primer framing and participant-situated cause/effect

Source URL: https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zGSGS1UHDogPKtvZB5hdT2A

> - introducing [The Young Lady’s Illustrated Primer](/zCjT6omFavtr7Zx2S5do6qC):
>   - [The Primer reminds people of their own experiences with profound enablement](/zKWgLsciawSbu25bqp28TGS)
>   - The Primer seems like a much better example than educational games: [The Primer’s goal is to produce creative, subversive youth](/zAQzpA63fdnYZKr9MqkePR3); it’s not about checking a bunch of learning objective boxes
>   - Like games, [The Primer is an enormous enacted experience](/zY9FkPizEf7uncVi5NdWyMc), and [The Primer is scalable](/zUwC5h2fhfpi9hqqU7ZHAQT)
>   - Unfortunately, the specific mechanics described in *The Diamond Age* wouldn’t work: see [The Primer isn’t a viable enabling environment](/z9R3ho4NmDFScAohj3J8J3Y) and subtree ==todo structure subtree in outline==
>   - it’s hard to imagine modifying educational games to become anything good, but the Primer is closer enough to the mark to be worth criticizing, and, possibly, to learn from
>   - Nell does actually feel like she’s bringing each step about; that’s not true in edu games because [Enacted experiences require participant-situated cause and effect](/zXEtC5KNkZ3kERSyyuJ9sy1)
>   - the Primer is about things that really matter ([The Primer’s goal is to produce creative, subversive youth](/zAQzpA63fdnYZKr9MqkePR3), even if Nell doesn’t know it ([Nell doesn’t know or share the Primer’s goals](/zP7aGQd3QJCntSPaEh2Swxg))
>   - activities in the Primer are much closer to an authentic enabling environment [Enacted experiences can bootstrap active participation in enabling environments](/zKubRcJXKjiCDPsPgJWckkP)
>   - it could be made continuous with expert environments, rather than something to leave behind ([The Primer is one big “onboarding” experience for the rest of Nell’s life](/zQns3rccKB1grnjrDjgd6Vs))
